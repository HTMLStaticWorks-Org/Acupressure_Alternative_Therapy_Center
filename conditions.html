A responsible guide

Questions people bring to a wellness conversation.

Learn how complementary practices sit alongside medical care—this guide does not diagnose or recommend treatment.

Common questions

Start with understanding, not assumptions.

People may ask about these concerns when exploring complementary therapies. A qualified medical professional should assess symptoms and advise on treatment.

01
Back discomfort

Persistent, severe or worsening pain needs medical assessment, especially after injury or with weakness, numbness or bladder/bowel changes.

02
Headaches and tension

New, sudden severe headaches or headaches with neurological symptoms require urgent medical assessment.

03
Neck discomfort

Seek medical advice for pain after injury, fever, weakness, numbness or symptoms that persist or worsen.

04
Sleep and stress concerns

Ongoing sleep difficulty, distress or changes in mood deserve appropriate support from a healthcare professional.

05
Foot discomfort

Swelling, wounds, loss of sensation, circulation changes or unexplained pain should be medically assessed before foot treatments.

06
General wellbeing

Complementary practices may be explored for relaxation or personal wellbeing, without assuming they treat an underlying disease.

When to seek urgent care

Chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, sudden weakness or facial drooping, severe sudden headache, major injury, uncontrolled bleeding or rapidly worsening symptoms need urgent medical attention. Do not use a symptom guide or complementary therapy to assess emergencies.

Questions worth asking

Make an informed decision.

Can complementary therapy replace medical treatment?

No. Do not stop prescribed medication or delay recommended medical assessment. Discuss complementary practices with your healthcare professional.

Are results guaranteed?

No. Outcomes vary, and claims of guaranteed cures or pain relief are not appropriate.

What if I have a medical condition?

Discuss your circumstances with a qualified healthcare professional before booking. Share relevant information with the practitioner through an appropriate private process.
